Decision Readiness

What is decision readiness?

Decision readiness is whether a decision has enough evidence, authority, capacity, funding alignment, and accountability to survive implementation.

It is the difference between having more information and having a decision structure that can actually hold.

IVA category line.

A decision is not ready until evidence, authority, capacity, funding, and accountability line up.

A decision-ready organization can answer seven questions.

The questions are simple. The hard part is getting the evidence, authority, people, money, obligations, and follow-through to sit in one usable structure.

  • What is being decided?The choice is specific enough that people know what action is being approved, rejected, delayed, or escalated.
  • Why now?The timing pressure, opportunity, obligation, risk, or deadline is visible.
  • Who owns it?The deciding body, decision owner, implementation owner, and support roles are clear.
  • What evidence supports it?The packet, report, analysis, or operating record supports the decision instead of burying it.
  • What resources does it require?Funding, time, staff capacity, leadership attention, vendor support, and operating changes are realistic.
  • What obligations shape it?Funder, public, legal, equity, stakeholder, reporting, and accountability obligations are represented.
  • How will implementation be tracked?The decision includes next actions, records, cadence, owners, and follow-through.

Diagnostic

The IVA Decision Readiness Score.

IVA scores readiness from 0 to 25 across five dimensions. The score is not a certificate. It is a practical diagnostic for finding the gap that keeps a decision from moving.

0-5

Evidence clarity

Is the evidence usable for this decision, or is it only informative background?

0-5

Authority clarity

Who can decide, who recommends, who implements, and who must be consulted?

0-5

Capacity realism

Can the organization execute the decision without hiding the labor or shifting the burden?

0-5

Funding alignment

Do the budget, assumptions, constraints, timing, and tradeoffs support the action?

0-5

Accountability clarity

Are obligations, records, owners, cadence, and implementation measures explicit?

Symptoms of low decision readiness.

Low readiness often looks like more work, not less. The organization keeps producing material because the decision structure is still incomplete.

Reports keep growing

The packet gets longer without becoming easier to decide from.

Meetings repeat

The same issue returns because authority, evidence, timing, and next action are split apart.

Implementation drifts

Approval happens, but owners, resources, routines, and accountability do not hold.

Capacity gets hidden

The work only moves because people absorb untracked labor, context rebuilding, and correction loops.

Funding assumptions float

The money, timing, constraints, and obligations are not tied to the actual decision.

External obligations sit outside the frame

Funder, public, equity, stakeholder, or compliance expectations shape the work without shaping the decision.

When to use IVA.

Use IVA when a board, funder, executive, public body, or mission-driven team has enough material to see the issue, but not enough structure to make the decision hold.
