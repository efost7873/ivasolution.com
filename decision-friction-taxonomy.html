Decision Friction Taxonomy

The terms for the decision problems organizations keep feeling but cannot always name.

Stuck decisions usually are not caused by one missing report or one difficult meeting. They come from repeatable friction patterns across evidence, authority, capacity, funding, obligations, and implementation.

Why name the pattern?

Naming the friction turns vague delay into a decision problem that can be mapped, scored, and acted on.

Core decision friction terms.

These terms are used by IVA to diagnose why a decision stalls even after reports, meetings, funding options, and plans already exist.

  • Decision debtUnresolved choices accumulate across meetings, reports, approvals, and workarounds until every next step carries old ambiguity.
  • Approval dragWork slows because authority, evidence, timing, and accountability are split across different people, packets, or forums.
  • Evidence gapThe decision lacks usable proof to support action, even when the organization has plenty of information.
  • Capacity leakHidden labor is required to keep broken processes functioning, but that labor is not represented in the decision.
  • Board packet driftMaterials expand without becoming more decision-ready for the board, committee, or public body that must act.
  • Implementation driftApproval happens, but owners, routines, funding, records, and accountability do not hold after the vote or signoff.
  • External obligation gapFunder, public, equity, compliance, stakeholder, or community obligations shape the work but are not structurally represented.
  • Ledger imbalanceOne value domain, usually finance, dominates the decision while operational, capacity, learning, external, or equity consequences remain informal.

How IVA uses the taxonomy.

The taxonomy is not a vocabulary exercise. It is a way to convert organizational frustration into a structured readiness diagnosis.

Define the pattern

Separate the decision problem from personality, effort, and vague complexity.

Build the artifact

Produce a packet map, decision memo, evidence map, obligation tracker, or next-action structure that can support action.

When to use this page.
